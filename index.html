Most programmes can describe what they delivered. Far fewer can show what changed.

Attendance registers and workshop photographs tell a funder that money was spent. They do not show whether a business is steadier, whether an owner can read their own numbers, or whether any of it will hold once the programme ends. CIMP was built to answer those questions with evidence.

Three indices, one score that moves

Every entrepreneur is scored on the same 67 statements, each rated from strongly disagree to strongly agree. The statements roll up into three indices, weighted by how much each one tells you about real progress.

ERI
30%
Entrepreneur Readiness Index
30 indicators — the owner
  • Entrepreneurial mindset
  • Business knowledge
  • Financial literacy
  • Compliance and governance
  • Planning and growth
  • Digital readiness
BHI
40%
Business Health Index
20 indicators — the business today
  • Revenue and profitability
  • Customers and market access
  • Financial management
  • Operations
  • Employment and local impact
SRI
30%
Sustainability & Resilience Index
17 indicators — life after the programme
  • Financial sustainability
  • Growth sustainability
  • Leadership sustainability
  • Operational sustainability
  • Environmental sustainability
  • Social sustainability
Aggregate Impact Index

The three indices combine into a single figure per entrepreneur and per cohort. It is the number a funder asks for, and because the weighting never changes, this quarter is comparable with the last one and with every other cohort you run.

Five measurement points, set by you

Each cohort is assessed on the same cadence, so movement is movement and not an artefact of when somebody happened to fill in a form.

Baseline
At intake

Demographics, documents and a first full assessment. Everything afterwards is measured against this.

Q1
Immediate outcomes

What shifted while the training was still fresh — confidence, record-keeping, pricing.

Q2
Early-stage outcomes

Whether the new habits survived contact with the business.

Q3
Intermediate outcomes

Revenue, customers and employment begin to show the difference.

Q4
Endline

The closing picture, and the evidence that the programme worked or did not.

Participants join by SMS

You send an invite to a phone number. They set a password, complete their intake and are on the system — no paperwork to capture afterwards.

Whole cohorts move together

Set the date each survey opens for a cohort and everyone starts it on the same day, whatever date they registered.

Reminders go out on their own

When a survey opens, the people who have not done it get an SMS. Anyone who has already finished is left alone.

Evidence in the form each audience needs

Beneficiary report

One entrepreneur's journey across every period, with the index breakdown, the narrative and the assessor's notes.

Client and cohort report

The funder-facing document: cohort movement, aggregate scores, demographics and the narrative that explains them.

Live dashboard

Where the cohort stands right now, which assessments are outstanding and how each index is tracking.

Client portal

Sponsors see the cohorts they fund, without access to anyone else's programme.

And the participants rate you back

The Programme Experience & Impact Index asks each entrepreneur to score the programme itself on five dimensions, every quarter.

  1. Programme design — the structure and content met my needs
  2. Mentorship and support — mentors were accessible and knowledgeable
  3. Administration and communication — scheduling and communication ran smoothly
  4. Practical application — the tools were usable in my business
  5. Overall experience — the programme was a positive one

Built for the way this data has to be handled

POPIA consent at intake

Every participant gives explicit, recorded consent before any personal information is stored, naming the organisation that holds it.

Documents stay private

ID and compliance documents upload into private storage. They are reachable only through the system, by people with a reason to see them.

People see only their own work

Participants reach their own surveys and nothing else. Administrators, assessors and sponsors each have their own view.

A phone number is enough

Participants sign in with the number you invited, on whatever handset they have. No email address required.
